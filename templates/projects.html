RISE Lab · Current research

A self-calibrating digital twin for UR5e manipulation

The goal is a real-to-sim-to-real loop: use observations from a physical UR5e to improve its simulated twin, then use that twin to plan or train manipulation controllers for the real robot.

Current progress: The single-arm workstation scene has been built and verified in simulation. The next milestones are repeatable scripted manipulation and characterization of the simulated arm.

Research direction: Calibrate the twin against real observations, investigate online adaptation, and explore model predictive control, imitation learning, and reinforcement learning for manipulation.

Status: Physical calibration, controller transfer, and real-robot validation are future work. The earlier two-arm simulation established experience with coordinated manipulation and remains a benchmark.

NAU undergraduate capstone · UGRADS

Shot difficulty and scoring across the NBA and NHL

How much of a player's shooting efficiency reflects the difficulty of the shots they take? This capstone uses matched 2014–2024 NBA and NHL shot data to study expected scoring, shot difficulty, and player performance.

Open full-resolution graph to zoom

My work: Developed data preparation and validation workflows, shot-quality models, a Shot Difficulty Index, and visual comparisons of player performance. The team presented the capstone at NAU UGRADS.
